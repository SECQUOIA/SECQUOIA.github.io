    <h2>Long Biography</h2>

    <p>
      David E. Bernal Neira is the Mitchel Papanicolas Family/Del Ray Glass Company Assistant Professor of Chemical Engineering at Purdue University. He leads the <a href="index.html">SECQUOIA</a> research group (Systems Engineering via Classical and QUantum Optimization for Industrial Applications) and co-directs the Center for Operations and Optimization in Process Systems (COOPS). His research develops theory, algorithms, and open-source software for discrete-continuous nonlinear optimization, and explores how quantum and other emerging computing architectures can address challenging problems in chemical and process systems engineering. His group applies these methods to energy and infrastructure systems, process intensification and control, computational chemistry, machine learning, and decision-making under uncertainty.
    </p>

    <p>
      Before joining Purdue, Bernal Neira was a research scientist at NASA's Quantum Artificial Intelligence Laboratory and an associate scientist at the Universities Space Research Association's Research Institute for Advanced Computer Science. He earned a Ph.D. in chemical engineering from Carnegie Mellon University and bachelor's degrees in chemical engineering and physics, as well as a master's degree in chemical engineering, from Universidad de los Andes. He is a member of the inaugural cohort of Purdue Engineering's Emerging Research Leaders and the Young Academy of the Colombian Academy of Exact, Physical and Natural Sciences.
    </p>
  </div>

  <p>David E. Bernal Neira is the Mitchel Papanicolas Family/Del Ray Glass Company Assistant Professor of Chemical Engineering at Purdue University, where he leads the <a href="index.html">SECQUOIA</a> research group and co-directs the Center for Operations and Optimization in Process Systems. He develops optimization theory, algorithms, and software and studies quantum and emerging computing methods for chemical, energy, and infrastructure systems.   <h2>Research Interests</h2>

  <p>Our group works at the interface of optimization, systems engineering, and advanced computing. Key research areas include:</p>

  <ul>
    <li>Optimization in systems engineering, including energy and infrastructure systems, process intensification, and control</li>
    <li>Quantum algorithms for combinatorial optimization in chemical and process systems engineering</li>
    <li>Optimization and simulation algorithms for emerging computing hardware</li>
    <li>Decision-making under uncertainty using optimization, machine learning, and artificial intelligence</li>
    <li>Theory, algorithms, and software for discrete-continuous nonlinear optimization</li>
  </ul>
