## Data Science in Chemical Engineering at Purdue University — Spring 2025, Spring 2026 (CHE 597)

Artificial intelligence and data-driven modeling are topics of growing importance across engineering. Rather than treating chemical engineering domain knowledge and computational data science as separate tracks, solving real engineering applications requires combining physical principles with modern mathematical and statistical tools. Prof. Bernal Neira teaches this course for senior undergraduates, master's students, Ph.D. researchers, and interested practitioners across campus, covering scientific programming, statistical inference, mathematical optimization, and machine learning. All lecture notebooks and examples from the inaugural offering are openly available in our group's [GitHub repository](https://github.com/SECQUOIA/PU_CHE597_S2025).

## Process Dynamics and Control at Purdue University — Fall 2023, Fall 2024, Fall 2025, Fall 2026 (CHE 456)

Prof. Bernal Neira teaches this core senior undergraduate chemical engineering course, introducing mathematical modeling, dynamic analysis, and feedback control of time-varying chemical processes. As students' primary course in transient process behavior, CHE 456 connects differential-equation modeling and system identification with practical controller design, tuning, and stability analysis for unit operations encountered across the process industries.

## Quantum Integer Programming and Machine Learning at Carnegie Mellon University — Fall 2020, Fall 2021, Fall 2022, Fall 2023 (47-779 / 47-785, 18-819F)

At Carnegie Mellon University, Prof. Bernal Neira co-designed and co-taught this interdisciplinary graduate course introducing discrete optimization, machine learning, and quantum computing for practitioners. Designed without requiring prior coursework in quantum physics, the syllabus connects integer programming and Ising/QUBO formulations with gate-model and annealing algorithms, culminating in a hands-on research project benchmarking quantum hardware and simulators against state-of-the-art classical solvers. Course materials remain available on the [Quantum Integer Programming and Machine Learning course website](https://bernalde.github.io/QuIPML22/).
